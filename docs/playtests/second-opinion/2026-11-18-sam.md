# Sam, 17 — keyboard-only second opinion

The core game, inspector, authoring form, and final choices were usable with a keyboard. Adding `cup` as an alias was the strongest teaching moment. I could not change the AI Interpreter selector from Off with the available keyboard automation, skipped Level 3, and passed Level 4 by saving a preference without experiencing AI. The card reported one skipped level, but that does not communicate how much AI practice was missing.

Sam is a fictional 17-year-old comfortable gamer who uses only a keyboard because precise mouse movement is difficult. I chose this persona to expose focus and keyboard traversal issues. This is a simulation, not real student data or a screen-reader test. Root operated the real published site and relayed visible state; I chose actions. All persona interactions used keys. Administrative reset/preflight preceded play; clean answers and 1280 × 800 were verified.

## Sticking points, expectations, and wording

### Level 1

I stayed in Drift. Initial Tab order was Learn, Play, Benchmark, Next, Drift, Hollow House, Temple, For Teachers, Command: nine presses. Learn had a clear teal outline. Focus did not disappear; disabled Continue was skipped.

`take pry bar` returned “I don't see any 'pry bar' here.” The description actually spelled it `prybar`, so this was my plausible spacing mistake. `take prybar` returned “You take the prybar.” `south` reached Cargo Bay. `talk droid` returned “'That crate needs leverage,' the droid says. 'A prybar was left in the cockpit.'”

`open crate` returned “You can't open the supply crate.” I expected holding the tool to be enough. `use prybar on crate` worked: “The prybar bites under the lid. With one hard pull, the crate snaps open around a glowing power cell.” `take power cell` returned “You take the power cell.” I went `north` and entered `use power cell on console`; the ship woke and the escape result showed seven turns. The complete victory paragraph was not retained verbatim.

Command retained focus throughout play, which helped. Backwards navigation to Continue after winning required twelve Shift+Tabs: Play again, nine history commands, For Teachers, Continue. History buttons are useful, but an efficient lesson-navigation route would help.

### Level 2

The task asked for `examine coffee mug`, then to click its command button. The reply was “I don't see any 'coffee mug' here.” Shift+Tab reached the latest command and Enter opened its inspector with a clear focus ring. The inspector showed word splitting, no typo correction, examine, an object search against prybar/nav console, no matching coffee mug, and AI off. Continue then enabled; Tab returned to Command. This made scenery versus authored objects clearer.

The instruction should mention Enter. An initial navigation detour was operator error: after transition, the next Tab continued near the old Continue position, though reported focus was the web area. Two Tabs would have reached Command; the operator assumed a top-of-page start, batched seven, then recovered. Do not count that detour as required product navigation.

### Level 3

The task requested a model, normal sentences, and impossible requests, with an understood request plus a question/guess outcome needed. The selector listed Off, Qwen 0.5B (1 GB), Llama 1B (1 GB), and recommended Qwen 1.5B (1.7 GB, described as getting eight in ten right).

Two Tabs reached the selector, with clear teal focus. End+Enter, Space, ArrowDown, and Alt+ArrowDown followed by End+Enter all left it Off with the menu closed. No download began. This is unresolved native-control/tool interaction, not proof of missing WebGPU, a failed download, or universally broken keyboard access. No mouse fallback was used. If this happened on my own device I would need help here.

After the probes below, Continue remained disabled. Eight Shift+Tabs from the last inspected command crossed earlier commands, model, and For Teachers to Next. Enter skipped the level.

### Level 4

The page warned that AI could not do anything until a model was chosen. Dial radios were reachable and Space selected them. Off was five Tabs from the focused top Next control. Reaching Command backwards took roughly two to five Shift+Tabs depending on the dial; newest-command inspection still worked reliably.

The model remained Off, so changing dials did not provide an AI comparison. After the probes below I selected Understand and saved “I want help with wording but I want the game rules to stay fixed.” The interface showed “SAVED”; Continue enabled while Fill gaps and Improvise tasks still said “Not done.” The success message said my AI boundary was set.

A forward route from Save reached Continue in eight Tabs: inspector scroll list, a stop with no reported page focus, Learn, Play, Benchmark, Previous, Next, Continue. The unexplained stop may be browser chrome/tool reporting, not a confirmed trap.

### Level 5

I retained `coffee mug`, described it as “A chipped blue mug with a tiny rocket painted on it.”, added alias `cup`, kept pickup checked, and activated Add to world. Feedback: “Added. Now examine it in the game.” Focus automatically returned to Command—excellent.

`examine cup` returned my exact description and enabled Continue. `take cup` returned “You take the coffee mug.” Now I understood why the earlier mug command failed and why the author makes an object real.

## Broken behavior versus uncertainty

Confirmed behaviors: history adds many backwards tab stops; Level 4 accepted a boundary without completed AI practice; Level 5 enabled progression before taking the mug. These are not necessarily implementation bugs. Level 4's completion signal is the strongest mismatch with the experience.

Unresolved: model selector keyboard attempts stayed Off. Reproduce by focusing it with Tab and trying End+Enter, Space, ArrowDown, or Alt+ArrowDown/End/Enter. A direct human keyboard check in the same browser is needed to distinguish tool limitations from product/browser behavior. The no-page-focus stops and transition overshoot also require that distinction. No confirmed keyboard trap was established.

## What Sam learned

“AI can help figure out my words, but putting a cup into the game is the author's job. I couldn't actually try the AI because I couldn't turn it on with these keys.”

The author/rules lesson landed through the cup exercise. The AI part remained something read rather than experienced. The final card should communicate that distinction.

## Top five fixes

1. Verify model selection with real keyboard input in supported classroom browsers; give clear selection/loading feedback and an actionable fallback. Diagnose our tool-specific uncertainty before declaring a site defect.
2. Distinguish saving a Level 4 opinion from completing AI practice; show missing practice on the final card.
3. Provide a short keyboard route from Command to Continue/lesson navigation without crossing all history commands.
4. Add “Tab to the command and press Enter” beside click instructions; make transition focus clear and predictable.
5. Improve recovery for likely spacing/context mistakes such as `pry bar` or `open crate` while holding the prybar.
